What actually goes wrong

Six ways a rushed takeover costs you later

None of these require bad accounting — just enough rows, and enough pressure to get to zero.

Unmapped rows still carry a balance

A skipped account isn't zero. It's money with nowhere to go, and it won't throw an error — it just won't be there when the trial balance is reviewed.

Many-to-one mappings need a paper trail

When two prior accounts roll into one of yours, someone eventually asks why. "I think that's what we did last time" isn't an answer.

A spreadsheet has no audit trail

Cell formulas don't record who assigned what, or when. If a mapping is ever questioned, there's nothing to point to.

A clean-looking export can still be wrong

A journal entry can import into Yardi without erroring even if the source never tied out. Reconciliation has to happen before export, not after.

Rushed takeovers get rushed exceptions

Go-live dates don't move for a messy chart of accounts. Under pressure, exceptions get "handled later" — and later rarely comes.

One-off mapping doesn't scale

Recreate the same account-by-account decisions for the next ten buildings, or keep the chart of accounts that already answers most of it.
